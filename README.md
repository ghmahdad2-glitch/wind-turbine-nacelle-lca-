# Life Cycle Assessment of a Wind Turbine Nacelle

## Project Overview

Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of the nacelle of an approximately 2 MW onshore wind turbine, conducted as part of an Electrical Engineering master's study project at Paderborn University.

The objective was to assess potential environmental impacts and identify the materials and manufacturing processes contributing most significantly to the environmental footprint.

## Tools and Methodology

- **Software:** openLCA
- **Database:** ecoinvent 3.12 – Cutoff System
- **Impact Assessment Method:** CML v4.8 (2016)
- **Reference:** Vestas V80, approximately 2 MW
- **Functional Unit:** One wind turbine nacelle, modeled mass approximately 62,400 kg

## System Boundaries

The model includes:

- Raw material production
- Component production and assembly
- Electricity consumption during production
- Road transportation

The use phase and end-of-life processes were not modeled in detail.

## Life Cycle Inventory

The inventory includes:

- Steel and cast iron
- Copper and aluminium
- Glass-fiber-reinforced plastics
- Polypropylene
- Electronic components and printed circuit boards
- Lubricating oil
- Electricity and transportation

The material quantities were based on simplified assumptions rather than a complete manufacturer bill of materials.

## Environmental Impact Assessment

Six environmental impact categories were evaluated:

1. Climate Change
2. Acidification
3. Eutrophication
4. Human Toxicity
5. Ozone Depletion
6. Photochemical Oxidant Formation

Detailed contribution analyses focused on Climate Change, Acidification, and Eutrophication.

## Key Findings

### Climate Change
Electronic components and printed circuit boards were the largest contributors, accounting for approximately 48% of the modeled climate-change impact. Steel followed with approximately 24%.

### Acidification
Copper was the dominant contributor, accounting for approximately 54% of the modeled acidification impact.

### Eutrophication
Electronic components and printed circuit boards accounted for approximately 54%, followed by copper with approximately 28%.

## Interpretation

The analysis demonstrates that environmental impacts are not determined solely by material mass.

Materials with relatively small mass contributions can have significant environmental impacts due to energy-intensive and emission-intensive production processes.

## Limitations

- Simplified material and process assumptions
- No complete manufacturer bill of materials
- Use phase not modeled in detail
- End-of-life and recycling scenarios not fully evaluated
- Scenario-dependent environmental results

The findings should therefore be interpreted as model-based estimates rather than verified environmental performance data for a specific manufactured nacelle.

Completed academic modeling and environmental impact analysis.

The detailed university report and underlying datasets are not publicly distributed in this repository.
